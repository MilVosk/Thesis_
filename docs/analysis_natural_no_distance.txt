Zero shot
-----------

Zero-shot runs with the natural-language prompt show the sharpest contrast between hallucinations and misses:

False Positives (55)

INFLUENCE dominates (41 cases). Without any few-shot anchors, the model treats every sentence that mentions “influence,” “contribute,” or “host” as an explicit relation, even when the text merely describes expected ecosystem behavior (e.g., resultszero_shot_false_positives.csv (line 3)).
HAVE FPs (9) include policy statements like “biodiversity depends on habitat” and dataset descriptions listing species traits, the same patterns seen in few-shot runs but even more pronounced here because no negative examples are available.
OCCUR_IN FPs (5) stem from catalog-like narratives (“The studied @ORGANISM$ @ENVIRONMENT$ contained 32 species”) where co-occurrence is mistaken for a location relation.
False Negatives (84)

HAVE sentences account for 49 misses; nearly every definition or measurement description is labeled NA (“The @QUALITY$ was calculated as…”) because the zero-shot prompt provides no guidance on interpreting formulaic expressions.
OCCUR_IN misses (25) include simple sampling statements (“Rain was captured in open @ENVIRONMENT$…”) that lack explicit verbs like “found,” so the model declines to predict a relation.
INFLUENCE misses (10) revolve around probabilistic or “important source” phrasing (“more likely to show vulnerability,” “play a vital role”) that the zero-shot instructions don’t categorize as causal.
Takeaways

Without few-shot context, the model overreacts to lexical cues (“influence”, “depends on”) and under-recognizes implicit relations. This underscores why the natural prompt needs curated negative examples and specialized HAVE/OCCUR_IN demonstrations: they directly address the dominant error modes seen in resultszero_shot_false_positives.csv and resultszero_shot_false_negatives.csv.
Any improvements should start by adding NA examples that explicitly say “depends on” but remain NA, plus HAVE/OCCUR_IN positives that reflect definition-style and passive constructions. Doing so in few-shot regimes already helps; applying similar adjustments would likely narrow the gap between zero-shot and few-shot performance.


Policy/Background Bias Persists – Across every natural-language few-shot setting (1‑2‑2, 2‑4‑3, 2‑4‑4, 4‑8‑8) most false positives stem from sentences that merely describe dependencies (“biodiversity depends on habitat”) or influence-context statements (“moisture influences fungi”). Even the largest NA pool (4‑8‑8) fails to curb these because the prompt still lacks NA examples that mirror that phrasing. Zero-shot results show the same pattern in extreme form (41 INFLUENCE FPs). Remedy: add explicit NA shots that contain verbs like “depends on”, “influence”, “contribute” so the model learns they can be NA unless an actual relation is asserted.

Definition-Style HAVE Is Chronically Missed – Every configuration lists HAVE as the dominant false-negative class (22/18/22/19/49 for 1‑2‑2, 2‑4‑4, 2‑4‑3, 4‑8‑8, zero-shot). These sentences express relations through formulas or comparative wording (“calculated as…”, “supported higher @QUALITY$”), which the natural prompt ignores. Without few-shots that label such phrasing as HAVE, recall will stay low regardless of few-shot counts.

Passive OCCUR_IN and Probabilistic INFLUENCE Need Coverage – Short sampling descriptions (“Rain was captured in…”) and probabilistic statements (“more likely to show vulnerability”, “play a vital role”) consistently become false negatives. None of the current few-shot pools demonstrate these patterns, so increasing NA counts does nothing; new positive examples must explicitly cover passive OCCUR_IN and softer INFLUENCE verbs.

Few-Shot Size Alone Doesn’t Solve Precision – Moving from 1‑2‑2 to 4‑8‑8 barely changes FP totals (45→50). The content of the examples matters more than the quantity. Targeted NA additions can tame the recurring hallucinations much more effectively than simply doubling sample counts.

Zero-Shot Reinforces Needed Adjustments – Without few-shot anchors, the prompt overfires on INFLUENCE (41 FPs) and misses every formula-style HAVE (49 FNs). That stark baseline underscores why the specific few-shot suggestions above—NA hedges, passive OCCUR_IN, derived HAVE—should be prioritized for all configurations.
